# User guide

This app is a Bible reader (BSB, WEB and KJV) with highlights, notes and a Saved list,
opened only with your code. It can look like an ordinary dictionary instead. It works with
no internet connection, ever.

## Two versions

Both versions have the same features and can look either like **Kanaiic Reader** (its own
name and icon, with a plain screen asking for your code) or like a **dictionary**. You can
switch between the two in Settings. They differ in how they start and where they come from:

- **From Google Play.** Starts as Kanaiic Reader and offers the dictionary disguise after you
  set your code. The app's Play Store page describes the disguise, and anyone who looks at the
  app's details on the phone can find that page.
- **Direct** (downloaded from kanaiic.com or GitHub). Starts already disguised as the
  dictionary, and it is not on any app store, so its details lead nowhere. If being found
  with a Bible app could put you in danger, use this one.

The phone's app settings (Settings > Apps) show one fixed name and icon whatever the app looks
like on the home screen: **"Dictionary"** on the direct version, and **"Kanaiic Reader"** on
the Google Play version. So on the Play version the disguise hides the app from a glance at
the home screen and app list, not from someone who checks the app's details.

Please read the section "What it does not protect against" before you rely on it. In some
places, being found with a hidden Bible app is more dangerous than being found with no Bible
app at all. Only you can weigh that.

## Before you install

- Decide whether carrying it is worth the risk where you are. The app makes it harder to
  find what you read and write. It does not make finding it impossible.
- Get the app only from someone you trust. A changed copy could do anything.
- After installing, delete the installer file (the .apk) from Downloads, messages or wherever
  it arrived. It sits there in plain view otherwise.
- If you installed with an app store or updater (for example Obtainium), that app lists
  where it came from. Remove the entry there once it is installed.

## Setting up your code

1. Open the app.
   - **Google Play version:** it asks you to choose a code straight away.
   - **Direct version:** it opens as a dictionary, with a message explaining how to get in.
     Tap **Set up now**, or hold the **Search** button for about two seconds; the setup
     screen opens. The message appears each time until a code is set.
2. Type a code of at least 6 digits, then the same code again. 8 or more digits is much
   better. Do not use your phone PIN, a birthday, or something like 123456.
3. Google Play version only: the app asks whether to disguise it now. **Disguise now** shows how to open it as a
   dictionary, then switches (the app goes to the home screen). **Later** leaves it as
   Kanaiic Reader; you can switch any time in Settings > Disguise. Either way, Settings always
   shows how to open the app, at the top.

There is no way to recover a forgotten code. Nobody can reset it, including the people who
made the app. If you forget it, everything inside is gone. Make a backup (see below).

## Opening and closing

As Kanaiic Reader: type your code on the number pad and tap **Open**.

As a dictionary, the way in is hidden in the **Search** button: hold it for about two seconds
(the search box clears), type your code in the box, then hold **Search** again. A normal tap
of Search just looks a word up.

If the code is wrong, nothing happens: the dictionary shows an empty search. If you type
anything other than digits in between, or wait 15 seconds, it goes back to normal and you can
start again.

The dictionary is a working dictionary and thesaurus with Notes and PDF tabs. Only its Search
button opens the reader; the Notes tab is an ordinary notepad, and the PDF tab opens PDF files
saved on the phone (tap **Open**, pick a file; pinch to zoom). Both are part of the disguise,
so they are **not** locked or encrypted: anyone using the dictionary can read them. Keep
private notes and documents inside the reader, not there. The app doesn't remember which PDFs
you opened, but the phone's file picker may list them under recent files.

It closes itself and shows the front screen again:

- when you tap **Hide** at the top left,
- when you double-tap the top-left corner,
- when you press Back on the main screen,
- when you go to the home screen or another app, or the screen turns off,
- after 60 seconds without a touch.

Screenshots, screen recording and the app-switcher preview are blocked while it is open.

## Reading

- Tap the book and chapter at the top to pick another. Recent passages are listed first.
- Swipe left or right for the next or previous chapter.
- Tap BSB (top right) to switch translation. You stay on the same passage.
- Search: type a reference like `John 3:16` or `1 cor 13:4-7` to go there, or words to
  search the current translation.
- Strong's: select verses and tap **Strong's** to see the Hebrew or Greek word behind each
  King James word, with its Strong's number. Tap one for its meaning and every verse that
  uses it. The **Strong's** tab searches the whole lexicon: type a number (`H430`, `G26`), an
  English word as the King James Version translates it (`love`), or a transliteration
  (`agape`).
- Settings: theme (Paper, Sepia, Dark, Black), text size and line spacing.

## Highlights, notes and Saved

- Tap a verse to select it. Tap more verses to add them (they need not be next to each
  other); tap a selected verse to remove it. Long-press a verse to add every verse from the
  last one you tapped.
- With verses selected: pick a color to highlight (✕ removes), Note to write a note, or Save.
- A note shows as a small dot after its verse. Tap the dot to read or edit it.
- At the end of each chapter you can add a note on the whole chapter, and save the chapter.
- The Saved tab has two parts. Verses: what you saved, each with a label and its own notes.
  Notes: every note you have written, with a button to open it in the reader.

Highlights and notes belong to the verse, so they show in every translation.

## Changing how it looks

Settings > Disguise switches between the app's own name and icon and the dictionary; the icon
and name change straight away, and the app returns to the home screen. The reader, your code and everything inside stay
the same, and the screen reminds you of the way in when you switch.

Earlier versions also offered other disguises. They were removed
in version 1.5: a disguise someone knows about is easy to recognise, so each extra one was a
weak point. If your phone used one of them, the update turns it into the dictionary.
Updating never undoes a disguise: a phone that looks like a dictionary keeps looking like one. Your code
and everything inside stay the same; open it by holding **Search** as described above.

## Backups

Settings > Backup > Export saves your notes, highlights and saved verses to a file locked
with a passphrase you choose. Import adds a backup's contents to the phone you are on. It
never deletes or replaces anything.

- Choose a passphrase of several unrelated words. You will need it to import. It cannot be
  recovered.
- The file looks like random data, has no tell-tale name or header, and only works with the
  passphrase. Still, keep it somewhere it will not draw attention, and not on the same phone
  if you can avoid it.
- The Bible text itself is not in the backup; it comes with the app.

## Erasing

- Settings > Erase everything deletes your code and everything inside, at once.
- Settings > Security > "Erase after 10 wrong codes" is off unless you turn it on. When on,
  10 wrong codes in a row erase everything. A right code
  resets the count. This protects against someone guessing on your phone, but a person who
  knows about it could also use it to destroy your notes. Keep a backup if you turn it on.
- Change code (Settings > Security) lets you pick a new code at any time.

## What it protects against

- Direct version, once switched to the dictionary: someone looking at your home screen, app
  list or app settings sees a dictionary called "Dictionary" with no permissions and no
  internet access. The Play version's app settings still say Kanaiic Reader and lead to its
  Play Store page.
- Someone using the disguise finds an app that works. Wrong codes leave no trace.
- Someone who copies your phone's storage gets encrypted data. The key is tied to your code
  and to this phone's security chip, so they cannot try codes on another computer.
- Your notes are not sent anywhere. The app has no internet permission at all.

## What it does not protect against

- Someone watching you type the code. In a disguise the digits show on the screen.
- Anything in the dictionary's Notes or PDF tabs. They are ordinary, unlocked features.
- On Kanaiic Reader, anyone who checks the app's details. See "Two versions".
- Someone who makes you open it, or who takes the phone while it is open.
- A skilled examiner with time. They can take the app apart, see that it hides a reader,
  and find the Bible text inside the app file (it is only lightly locked there). They can
  also try codes on your actual phone, which is why a long code matters and why the
  10-wrong-codes option exists.
- Your keyboard app. The app asks keyboards not to learn what you type in notes, but some
  keyboards ignore that. A keyboard that sends typing to the internet can leak notes.
- A phone that is already compromised by spying software.
- Signs outside the app: the installer file, an updater's or app store's list, a backup file someone finds
  and recognizes, or people who know you use it.

It reduces risk; it does not remove it.

## If your phone is taken

- If it was closed, what you read and wrote is encrypted. Do not open it for anyone if you
  can avoid it.
- If you can, turn the phone off. Encrypted phone storage is strongest while the phone is off.
- Your backup file and passphrase let you restore your notes on another phone.
